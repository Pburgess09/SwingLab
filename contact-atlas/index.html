03 / ANGLE AT CONTACT

How does height change the angle?

Attack angle describes the bat sweet spot’s vertical direction at impact relative to horizontal. Positive means upward; negative means downward. It is not the ball’s launch angle or the swing-plane tilt. These are observed successful hits—not prescribed targets.

04 / THE COACHING QUESTION

The cue is personal.
The movement is measurable.

Working hypothesis: a “down” feel may help a hitter flatten an overly upward approach to a high pitch; an “up” feel may help produce a more upward approach to a lower pitch. A flatter measured path does not necessarily mean a downward path. These records contain no cue reports, so they cannot establish which thought was needed or effective. Test the cue against that hitter’s measured response, including unsuccessful attempts.

MEASUREMENT NOTES

Read the coordinates correctly.

This graph filters the existing imported Statcast data to pitch_type FF and events single, double, triple or home_run, with description hit_into_play. It excludes pitch centers outside the 17-inch plate width or the individualized vertical strike zone, and excludes missing contact depth. It is not a batting-average chart.

Horizontal position uses plate_x converted to batter-relative inside/outside. Height is normalized as (plate_z − sz_bot)/(sz_top − sz_bot). Depth uses intercept_ball_minus_batter_pos_y_inches, the contact displacement ahead of the hitter’s center of mass. Pitch position is not ball position at contact. No guessed translation to home plate is applied.

The median view defaults to an expanded 18–36 inch depth axis so small differences are easier to see. The full-range option spans −5 to 65 inches. Both batting sides use the same scale. Individual-hit mode always uses the full range to retain every displayed observation. Only the depth-axis display scale changes; measurements, colors and summaries are unchanged.

The grid contains five horizontal and seven vertical bands. Sphere positions use cell centers and the median measured depth. The middle 50% is the interquartile range, not a confidence interval. Individual-hit mode shows a deterministic sample of up to 1,400 hits per batting side. Medians use all eligible observations. Sparse cells remain visible with their sample size.

Left/right switching uses independently observed groups, not mirrored copies of the same hitters. No expected inside-to-outside pattern is imposed. The lab animation is a separate dataset and does not drive this graph.

Attack angle comes directly from the Statcast attack_angle field, independently of launch_angle. Cell depth and angle are separate medians, not one reconstructed swing. Missing angles remain unavailable and their counts are shown. The small side-view arrows illustrate only the vertical angle relative to horizontal; they do not reconstruct full bat paths or infer horizontal attack direction. The expanded depth axis does not alter these angle sketches.
